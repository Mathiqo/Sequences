What is a Term-to-Term Rule?

A sequence is a pattern of numbers that follows a specific rule[cite: 1]. Each number in the sequence is called a term.

A term-to-term rule tells you how to get from one term to the next term[cite: 1].

Example Sequence: 1, 2½, 4, 5½, 7, ...[cite: 1]

Notice that each number increases by 1½ relative to the previous number[cite: 1]. Therefore, the term-to-term rule is "add 1½"[cite: 1].

Two-Step Rules & Reversing Operations

Some rules combine two operations, such as "multiply by 2 and add 5"[cite: 1]. You can also work backwards using inverse (opposite) operations[cite: 6]!

Example (Forward): First term = 3. Rule = Multiply by 2, then add 5[cite: 1]

  • 2nd term: $3 \times 2 + 5 = 11$[cite: 1]
  • 3rd term: $11 \times 2 + 5 = 27$[cite: 1]
  • 4th term: $27 \times 2 + 5 = 59$[cite: 1]
